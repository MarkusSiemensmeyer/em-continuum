AI speeds up building.
Let’s speed up understanding.

Why a shared picture of our workflows is what makes AI pay off in how we build software.

Open on common ground, not on a method. Nobody in the room has to be convinced that AI matters — the question is how we get real value out of it in software delivery without turning the organization upside down.

We don’t need to discuss whether to use AI.
The question is where it pays off.

What AI changes

Writing code, tests and documentation becomes fast and cheap.

What it doesn’t change

Someone still has to know precisely what the software should do.

What follows

The value moves to the part before the code: understanding and agreeing.

This is the hinge of the whole argument. AI is a multiplier on the building part. It does not resolve ambiguity in what business wants — it resolves it silently, by guessing.

“That’s not what we meant.”

Cost of fixing a misunderstandingfound late
Nobody in this chain does a bad job; the loss accumulates at each hand-off. AI does not remove a hand-off — it is one more translator, a very fast one. A misunderstanding now reaches the end of the chain sooner and in larger volume.

AI won’t fix these on its own.

Ask: which of these have we seen in the last twelve months? These are cost, schedule and capacity issues. All of them start before the first line of code — exactly where AI does not help by itself.

In one sentence

Make the flow of information visible — and find gaps and misunderstandings before anyone builds.

Event Modeling is a simple, disciplined way to draw how information moves through a workflow, so that everyone can read it and nothing important stays implicit.

This is the slide to remember. If someone only takes one thing away, it should be this sentence — not the name of the method.

Benefits on two levels.

For IT leadership

  • Predictable deliveryScope is visible up front — including the parts nobody mentioned.
  • Progress you can showWorking steps instead of percentages, in every management meeting.
  • AI under controlGenerated work is anchored in a reviewed picture, not in a prompt.
  • Less dependency on individualsKnowledge lives on the board, not in a few heads. Onboarding gets faster.

For the company

  • Less reworkMisunderstandings are fixed on a whiteboard, where they are cheap.
  • AI investment pays offFaster building translates into faster right results.
  • Business and IT, one languageDecisions are made on the same picture, not via translations.
  • Safer modernizationExisting behaviour is made visible before it is replaced.
Left column: arguments for the IT lead herself. Right column: arguments she can take to her managers. None of them requires anyone to understand the notation.
